# Project brief for an AI: Clarity

Read this first if you are an AI starting work on this project. It is a self-contained summary of what is being built, why, what exists, and what the rules are. The full spec is `LIFE-SYSTEM.md`; the build instructions are `../CLAUDE.md`. If they ever disagree with this brief, they win.

## 1. In one paragraph
Clarity is a private Android app for one person at a time, whoever installs it. It is their lifetime life system: it holds their goals, plans, daily results, patterns and every conversation in an encrypted on-device database, for years, and lets them talk with an AI coach that has all of that in front of it. The app ships knowing nothing about its user; the coach learns who they are only from what they say and do. It is not a chatbot. The coach is wise, honest and adaptive; the app around it is reliable, boring and mostly code.

## 2. The person
Anyone who wants to become a better version of themselves on purpose: someone with goals, studies or work to plan, habits to build, or something hard to work through. They want deep, honest, wise answers (no length cap), not generic coaching. The design exists to prevent the ways earlier coaching apps fail: a coach that says "just do one lesson" when the numbers cannot reach the deadline; invented facts about the user; claiming things were saved when they were not; bugs.

## 3. The core rule
**The AI only talks. The app changes state only when the owner taps a button.** The AI can show cards (a suggested promise, plan, target change, backlog, focus, note, or chat-type switch) at the end of a reply. Nothing is saved until they tap the card's button. There are no hidden actions. Background AI jobs never create cards and never change goals, plans, targets or promises.

Second rule: **code does all math, dates and counting.** The AI is handed finished facts (days left, required pace, their typical pace, their best fortnight, where each reaches, review-date options) and copies them. It never calculates.

## 4. What the app does (features)
**Talk tab.** One chat component with a chat-type dropdown: Free talk, Untangle (make sense of something tangled, no commitments), Decide (decision support, never picks for them), Plan and track (the planner coach, sees their tracker data), plus one entry per active Focus. Many conversations, one shared memory. Conversation drawer with search, rename, delete, "Forget this conversation". Off-the-record chats are never saved. Replies stream, survive leaving the app, and are saved as they arrive. A due card at the top shows promises and unanswered check-ins. A yellow-outlined letter card appears when a new weekly letter is unread.

**Plan tab.** Sections: Today (daily check-in per target: Did it / Part / Not, an amount stepper, reason chips for misses, rest days), Goals (paste or write a goal of any horizon; each shows its plan version, targets, pace status), Promises (one-off commitments with optional reminder; outcomes Done, Not done, Moved, Let go), Reviews (weeks and months, with the weekly letter), and a "Plan with coach" button that opens the planner chat loaded with the relevant numbers. Backlogs (a finite amount by a deadline, such as 40 lectures by 20 Nov) show required versus actual pace.

**What I know tab.** Everything the app has learned, readable, editable and deletable: Profile, Patterns, What helps, What does not help, Open threads, People, Focuses, Chapters. Each item shows its evidence (their own quotes, dates, counts). Their deletions are final.

**Focus.** A long-running topic (for example public speaking, a final exam) with a rolling summary the AI rewrites after each chat in it, so months-long work never resets. Journeys such as "overcoming social anxiety" are simply a Focus plus a plan with graded steps.

**Reviews and letters.** Weekly review (numbers computed in code plus a short AI note), monthly review, quarterly chapter, and a weekly letter (on by default, Sunday evening) written from the week's conversations and check-ins. Letters appear as a card on Talk, a dot on Plan, and a list under Plan, Reviews. Every quote in a letter is verified word for word against what they wrote, and every number against the inputs, or the letter is not published.

**Settings.** Provider, base URL, API key, chat model name and background model name (typed freely; defaults only in one config file), backup provider (auto-failover after 3 failures), reminders and keep-alive settings for HyperOS, app lock, encrypted automatic weekly backup to a folder they pick, readable Markdown+JSON export, erase everything, and an Advanced section (embedding model and Re-index, prompt editor with Reset to built-in, test bench, health, error log without content).

**Not in v1** (Later list): growth tree, ledgers, screen-time tracking, journeys as a separate feature, onboarding questionnaires, practice-a-conversation mode, multi-language, cost budgets, widgets, read-aloud.

## 5. The tracker and planner (code-side)
Goals have plans (versioned); plans have targets (recurring, with a minimum for bad days and a target for normal days, a schedule such as `Sat-Thu`) and backlogs. A check-in is one row per target per day with a level (rest, not, minimum, target, beyond), amount, reason chip and optional note. A missing row is "no answer", never a miss. No streaks. Computed per target and backlog: remaining, scheduled days left, required per day, typical pace (last 14 days), best fortnight so far, where each pace reaches, answer rate; weekly stats; a re-plan flag when two weekly reviews in a row project below 85% of what remains. Pace is judged against what they have actually done, not what they hope to do. Shown numbers never flatter the plan (required pace rounded up, "reaches" rounded down), and a code-written Status line tells the coach whether the total is reached at their typical pace, only at their best fortnight, or not at all. All of it is pure code, unit-tested with hand-checked vectors (`tools/planner.py`, `contracts/planner_vectors.json`).

## 6. The coach (prompts)
All prompts are in `prompts/` and are bundled into the app. The persona (`persona.md`) runs five private checks before each reply (what they need, the real question, what it knows and how well, the other side, what to leave out); adapts to their energy, state, readiness, the hour, their family context, and the notes about what helps or does not help them; holds its ground without winning, gives the same assessment in any mood, hears the other side in conflicts, checks "0 to 10, how much do you want this?" before a promise, plans for obstacles with if-then, treats a missed item as information, never answers a deadline problem with only a tiny start, has a short safety rule if they may be in danger, and lands a conversation after about twenty turns. Chat types add `mode_*.md`; `cards.md` defines the card contract; `examples.md` has 21 fictional demonstrations (deliberately unlike their real life, so the model cannot mistake them for facts about them); `reflection.md`, `review_weekly.md`, `review_monthly.md`, `letter_weekly.md`, `chapter.md` and `gardening.md` drive background jobs; `testbench.md` has 69 scenarios with automatic checks.

## 7. Memory (built for a lifetime)
Archive of everything forever (encrypted, searchable, never sent in full). Notes (pattern, what helps, what does not, strength, value, thread) each with a confidence grade (guess, likely, confirmed) and evidence rows. Only their own words and actions are evidence; a coach claim never raises confidence; a pattern needs two separate occurrences. Profile of at most about 30 durable entries. Quarterly chapters replace old summaries in context. Retrieval is keyword (FTS4) plus meaning (on-device embeddings, bge-small-en-v1.5), merged and re-ranked; embeddings only find, never write. A bounded context packet is sent each time (`contracts/context.md`, `tools/context.py`): a cached fixed prompt, frozen context blocks (about 9k tokens), a history window (about 24k), and volatile facts (about 3k: time, plan numbers, promises, card results) next to their new message; anything from them or the model is escaped before entering it.

## 8. Reliability and safety design
Small surface (3 tabs, one chat component, 7 card types, 29 tables). One writer per fact (repository commands, one transaction each). Fail closed at every AI boundary (card parser, JSON appliers, QuoteCheck, NumberCheck), each with an executable Python reference, shared JSON vectors and a mutation check that proves the vectors catch planted bugs. Pure core logic tested on the JVM against shared JSON vectors, so Kotlin and the Python reference specs cannot drift. Explicit state machines with startup repair. Idempotent background jobs (unique job keys). Derived data rebuildable. Day key with a 04:00 boundary so history survives time-zone changes. Prompt contract tests. Versioned database, migration tests, never destructive migration, verified encrypted backups, readable export. No analytics, no accounts, no content in logs; only calls to the AI provider leave the phone. CI gate on every push; signed release APK from a version tag.

## 9. Technology
Kotlin, Jetpack Compose (Bold dark theme, Space Grotesk), Hilt, Room + SQLCipher with a Keystore key, OkHttp SSE streaming (DeepSeek by default, OpenAI-compatible, Anthropic as backup), WorkManager, foreground ReplyService, exact alarms for reminders, ONNX Runtime embeddings. Much of it can be reused from the old repo `r8646582-cell/clarity-` (see `../CLAUDE.md`); that repo is a read-only reference and no old data is imported.

## 10. What exists today in this repo
Finished and passing (`bash tools/check_all.sh`): the spec; all 14 prompts; executable references with shared vectors for card parsing, planner math, quote and number checks, the context packet, the background appliers and the test bench checks, plus state machines and a mutation check; the database schema (`db/schema.sql`); the CI workflow; `CLAUDE.md`. `CHANGES.md` lists what the October 2026 review fixed and why. UI mockups exist on a design canvas (not in the repo). **Not built:** the Android app itself. Nothing has been run on a device or on a real model yet.

## 11. Build order
A skeleton and chat; B memory; C promises and cards; D planner, reviews, letters; E focuses, chapters, backup, export, health, test bench; F two weeks of real use, then v1 is final. Details and "done when" criteria are in `../CLAUDE.md`.

## 12. How to work with the owner
Plain language, no jargon without explanation, step-by-step instructions for anything they must do themselves. Tell them honestly what you could not run or verify. Ask before adding dependencies or changing scope. Do not claim something works unless it was tested. They have authorised rewriting any prompt or part of the app, but changes to prompts and contracts must keep every check passing.

## 13. Decisions already made (do not reopen without asking)
Bold visual design with bubbles for both sides; three tabs; start clean (no old data import); strong model with thinking on for every message and no reply length cap; model names are typed settings; bge-small embeddings; weekly letter on by default; optional silent Sunday alert; day boundary 04:00; no streaks; no help-line screens (the persona keeps a short safety rule); the plan is final once v1 passes two weeks of use, and new ideas go on the Later list.

## 14. Glossary
Card: a suggestion the app shows with buttons; nothing is saved until tapped. Focus: a long-running topic with a rolling summary. Check-in: one answer per target per day. Target: a recurring item with minimum and target levels. Backlog: a finite amount to clear by a deadline. Typical pace: their average over the last 14 answered days. Best fortnight: their best 14-day pace so far. Review options: dates the app offers for a plan's review. Part 1 / Part 2 / Part 3: the stable prefix, the context blocks and the per-message volatile data of the context packet. Test bench: scenarios run on the real model with automatic checks plus human judgement.
